# Evaluation round: July 2026

**Scope.** An end-to-end evaluation of BioFuzz against its stated goal — *use
fuzzing techniques to find new drugs, or new purposes for old drugs* — carried
out on the version of the code current in July 2026. Every finding below is
backed by a measurement, not by reading the source.

**Evidence base.** One real campaign against `hiv_protease` (~2 hours, 1 worker,
CPU only) plus direct docking of all five bundled targets' reference drugs, which
had never been measured through the pipeline before this round.

**Outcome.** All findings in sections A–G were acted on. The conclusions stand;
some of the *numbers* in this document have since been re-measured, and where a
later round revised one it is marked inline. Read this as a record of a round,
not as current documentation — for that, see
[`../modules/`](../modules/) and the [README](../../README.md).

---

## Summary

The architecture held up. Module boundaries were clean, the analogy was well
chosen, and 92 tests passed throughout. The problems were not structural:

**Mechanisms the design depended on were not connected, and thresholds were set
by intuition rather than measurement.**

In three of the most damaging cases the module documentation already specified
the correct behaviour and the implementation had diverged from it:

| Spec said | Code did |
|---|---|
| `seeds.md`: "when a seed is first popped and docked, that's its calibration" | Seeds were never docked at all (B3) |
| `mutator.md`: "it skips to havoc on future selections" | Havoc was unreachable; splice always won (B1) |
| `corpus.md`: priority is "recomputed on pop" | Priority was fixed at insert (B2) |

Two documents contained outright errors that the implementation faithfully
reproduced: `oracle.md` claimed gnina reports strain in pose REMARKs (it does
not, A2), and `seeds.md` recommended rewarding high molecular weight — the exact
docking artifact that needs correcting (B4). Both documents were fixed alongside
the code, on the grounds that a future rebuild would follow them.

The tests passed throughout because they tested the code that existed, not the
behaviour the design called for. That is the durable lesson of this round.

### What the campaign produced

Ten findings in two hours:

- **All 10 were one molecule series** — sildenafil analogs, 5 unique parents, a
  single triage cluster
- **9 of 10 carried chemically impossible groups** (`-O-N(H)-O-`, `-O-CH2-O-H`)
- **1 of 10 was a literal duplicate** of another finding
- **Every one recorded `strain: null`** and `selectivity_ratio: null` — two
  oracle tiers never executed at all
- The run left **no `corpus/state.json` and no `coverage.json`**. Two hours of
  docking produced nothing resumable.

Meanwhile, measuring the five reference drugs for the first time showed that
**one target's hit threshold was unreachable by its own reference drug**, **two
targets silently rejected their own reference drug before docking**, and **the
conventional ligand-efficiency floor rejects most of them**.

That combination — everything the tool found was unusable, and much of what it
was built to find could not have been found — is the finding of this round. None
of it was visible from reading the code.

## A. Scoring

### A1. gnina's CNN scores were parsed and discarded

The stated reason for the engine choice was gnina's CNN scoring function. Its
mode table has five columns:

```
mode |  affinity  |  intramol  |    CNN     |   CNN
     | (kcal/mol) | (kcal/mol) | pose score | affinity
-----+------------+------------+------------+----------
    1       -9.81        1.30       0.3207      6.795
```

`parse_log` matched all five and kept **column 2 only**. So every dock paid CNN
inference cost — the dominant cost of the campaign — and then ranked, gated and
reported on vina's empirical score, the exact function gnina was chosen to
improve on.

**Change:** `oracle/scoring.py`, with a configurable policy defaulting to
`consensus` (the weaker of vina and the CNN, so a molecule must convince both).

### A2. The strain tier could never fire

`extract_strain` searched the *pose PDBQT* for a `REMARK ... INTRA` line. gnina
writes no such line; its pose REMARKs are `minimizedAffinity`, `CNNscore`,
`CNNaffinity` and `SMILES`. The intramolecular energy was available the whole
time, in column 3 of the table `parse_log` was discarding.

**Change:** strain is read from `DockingMode.intramol`; the pose-REMARK regex
survives only as a fallback for other engines.

### A3. Raw affinity selects for molecular size

Docking scores grow roughly linearly with heavy-atom count, so gating on
affinity alone selects large, greasy molecules regardless of contact quality.
Every single finding carried both `low_ligand_efficiency` and
`lipinski_mw_violation`. Ligand efficiency *was* computed — but only in triage,
after the campaign had already spent its whole budget climbing the
molecular-weight gradient.

**Change:** ligand efficiency became an in-loop oracle tier. It costs one
heavy-atom count, so there was never a performance reason to defer it.

### A4. Fuzz-stage docking was noisy enough to manufacture hits

At `exhaustiveness_fuzz: 4` with one dock per molecule, initial versus confirmed
affinity from the triage report:

| Initial | Confirmed | Δ |
|---|---|---|
| -10.46 | -9.38 | 1.08 |
| -10.01 | -8.06 | **1.95** |
| -10.21 | -9.60 | 0.61 |

A substantial share of "hits" was search noise.

**Change:** `exhaustiveness_fuzz` 4 → 8. Only a partial fix; see
[What was not changed](#what-was-not-changed).

## B. The corpus scheduler

### B1. Havoc was unreachable

The stage rule was "deterministic on first selection, else splice if a donor
exists, else havoc". `sample_donor` returns `None` only when the corpus holds
fewer than two entries, which after loading 250 seeds never happens. **Havoc was
dead code** — 9 findings came from the deterministic stage, 1 from splice, 0 from
havoc. AFL++ spends most of an input's budget in havoc; it is the primary
discovery mechanism.

**Change:** splice is gated behind a 25% probability and havoc is the default.

### B2. Mode collapse — 250 seeds in, one molecule explored

The priority heap was purely greedy with no diversity term. Every mutant of a
good molecule is itself a good molecule, so it re-entered at high priority and
crowded everything else out. Novelty (C1) was pinned near maximum for almost
every pose, flattening the one term that could have differentiated entries. The
reuse penalty was `times_fuzzed * 0.1` against a novelty weight of `10.0`, so an
entry had to be fuzzed **200 times** before falling behind a fresh sibling.

**Change:** a scaffold-crowding discount applied at selection time, priority
split into `base_priority` (intrinsic) and the crowding term, and the reuse
penalty raised to 1.0.

### B3. Seeds were never docked, so the repurposing use case did not exist

The most consequential finding of the round. The loop docked *mutants*; a corpus
entry was never docked itself. **BioFuzz never measured whether an approved drug
binds the target.** For a tool whose stated purpose includes repurposing, the
primary experiment was absent: "does this drug inhibit this protein?" was never
asked, only "do its mutants?".

It also broke the scheduler. Seeds kept `best_affinity = None` forever, so the
affinity bonus was 0 and the power schedule ranked 250 seeds on no evidence.

The "no seed triage" design decision was right — seeds should not need a blocking
pre-docking phase. AFL++ does not skip seeds either; it *calibrates* them on
first execution, and `seeds.md` said so twice. The distinction was lost between
document and loop: "no pre-docking phase" was implemented as "no docking".

**Change:** `Campaign._calibrate()`.

### B4. Seed priority rewarded molecular weight

```python
mw_bonus = max(0.0, (mw - 350.0) / 100.0) * 2.0
```

Unbounded in MW, so the heaviest seed in the file was always popped first. That
is how sildenafil (MW 475) became the molecule the campaign spent two hours on,
and it compounded A3: prior and oracle were biased toward size in the same
direction.

Here the *document* was the root cause. `seeds.md` said "higher MW within range →
modest priority bonus (bigger molecules tend to score better)". The parenthetical
is true, and that is exactly why the rule is wrong: bigger molecules score better
as a **docking artifact**, so a prior that rewards size amplifies the bias the
pipeline most needs to correct. "Within range" was never implemented as a range
either.

**Change:** a band centred on 350 Da, `1 - |mw - 350| / 200`.

## C. Coverage

### C1. Novelty carried almost no information

The whole fingerprint was hashed to **one** slot. Two consequences:

1. **No partial credit.** Contacting `{A,B,C}` and `{A,B,C,D}` produce unrelated
   slots, so "this pose reached a residue nothing had reached before" — precisely
   the AFL++ new-edge signal the design is built on — was inexpressible. The
   config key was named `priority_new_bit_weight`; there were no bits.
2. **Almost everything scored `strong`.** A 256 KiB map holds 262,144 slots, so
   nearly every distinct binding mode hashed somewhere unseen. Novelty pinned at
   maximum carries no signal, and it fed straight into B2.

`occupancy_rotate_threshold: 0.55` needs roughly 144,000 distinct fingerprints
to rotate an epoch, unreachable at CPU docking speeds; measured occupancy after a
real run was **0.000**, making the epoch mechanism decorative.

**Change:** a second, never-reset **union map** indexed by residue rather than by
hash, with `strong` novelty defined by new union bits, plus a non-saturating
rarity term. The combination map was kept as the weaker signal.

## D. Chemistry

### D1. Nothing checked whether molecules could exist

The mutation gate checked MW, logP, HBD, HBA and rotatable bonds. None of those
says anything about chemical plausibility. `atom_scan` walks sildenafil's ethoxy
tail one atom at a time, mutating C→N and C→O independently, producing
`-O-N(H)-O-` and `-O-CH2-O-H`: a hydroxylamine ether and a hemiacetal, not
molecules anyone could order, make or store. They docked well, passed every
filter, and became the campaign's top-ranked findings.

**9 of the 10 findings carried one of these two motifs.**

**Change:** `mutator/alerts.py`, a deliberately narrow catalog of unstable
motifs. RDKit's BRENK catalog was evaluated for this job and rejected: it flags
aspirin and every aniline.

### D2. Two targets silently rejected their own reference drug

The `molecules:` property bounds were global-only — one envelope for every
target — and preparation applies them, so a molecule outside the envelope is
dropped before docking, silently.

| Target | Reference | MW | logP | Under global bounds |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| braf_v600e | vemurafenib | 489.9 | **5.54** | **rejected** (max_logp 5.0) |
| egfr_kinase | erlotinib | 393.4 | 3.41 | ok |
| hiv_protease | indinavir | **613.8** | 2.87 | **rejected** (max_mw 550) |
| parp1 | talazoparib | 380.4 | 2.63 | ok |
| sars_cov2_mpro | nirmatrelvir | 501.6 | 1.22 | ok |

**Two of five targets could not process the drug they were built around.** The
consequence is larger than a wasted iteration: HIV protease inhibitors are
peptidomimetics (indinavir 614 Da, saquinavir 671, ritonavir 721) and type-II
kinase inhibitors are lipophilic by nature, so a 550 Da / logP 5.0 envelope
excludes **the entire chemical class that works on those targets**. No campaign
against `hiv_protease` could have found an indinavir-like molecule, because
nothing resembling one survives preparation.

This surfaced only by running the tool: a one-iteration smoke run reported
`docks=0` and `best_affinity=None`.

**Change:** `molecules:` is overridable per target, both affected targets set
their own bounds, `tests/test_calibration.py` guards the property, and a
preparation failure on a corpus entry is now logged at WARN rather than passing
silently. Viable chemical space is a property of the target, not a global
constant; ligand efficiency, not a blanket MW cap, is the instrument for keeping
size honest.

## E. Operations

### E1. Two hours of compute, nothing resumable

`checkpoint_every: 500` counts *iterations*, and one iteration is a whole batch
of roughly 20 docks, so 500 iterations is on the order of days on CPU. The run
never reached it: no corpus checkpoint, no coverage checkpoint, no `[CHKPT]` line
in the log. There was also **no `--resume` flag at all**, making a campaign
inherently all-or-nothing — the opposite of AFL++, where the queue is durable by
design and campaigns run for days across restarts.

**Change:** a wall-clock checkpoint interval alongside the iteration count, and
`--resume`.

### E2. Wasted docking

- **No dedup.** A molecule already docked was docked again when a mutation
  rediscovered it, and docking is essentially the whole cost of the loop.
- **Pool churn.** The `ProcessPoolExecutor` was created and destroyed every
  iteration: full interpreter start-up and RDKit import per worker, per batch.
- **Serial prep.** Embedding plus MMFF optimisation ran single-threaded on the
  main process while the worker pool sat idle.

**Change:** an evaluated-SMILES set, one pool for the campaign, and preparation
moved into the pool.

### E3. Duplicate findings

Findings `000001` and `000006` were the same molecule at -10.24 and -10.25.
Nothing deduplicated them, so the hit was double-counted and triage re-docked it.

**Change:** `FindingsStore` dedupes by SMILES and indexes what is already on disk
at construction, so the guarantee survives `--resume`.

## F. Triage

### F1. Selectivity used a ratio of energies, which is a unit error

```python
ratio = abs(record.confirmed_affinity) / abs(modes[0].affinity)
```

Binding free energies are kcal/mol on a **log** scale, so their ratio is not a
physical quantity. "-10 ÷ -5 = 2× selective" is dimensionally meaningless; the
real statement is a 5 kcal/mol gap, roughly 4000× in K<sub>d</sub>. Selectivity
is a difference, ΔΔG.

**Change:** `selectivity_ddg` plus a derived `selectivity_fold`.

### F2. Selectivity never ran anyway

It requires `offtarget_receptor` and `offtarget_box`, and no bundled target
defines them, so every record returned `selectivity_not_configured`. For a
repurposing tool, where "does this also hit something it shouldn't?" is a central
question, the discriminator was inert. **Still true** — see below.

## G. Configuration

### G1. Per-target docking overrides were silently discarded

`merge_defaults` assigned `docking` straight from the global config, so a target
could set its own exhaustiveness or CNN model and be ignored.

**Change:** every overridable section is merged key by key, with a regression
test.

### G2. Thresholds were set from literature, not from this pipeline

Per-target `affinity_threshold` values came from published affinities. A
threshold is only meaningful in the units the tool actually measures, and
measuring the reference drugs showed:

- **parp1's threshold was unreachable.** Set to -11.0 from a literature value of
  about -12.2, while talazoparib itself reaches only -10.45 here. The target's
  own reference drug could not have been called a hit, and neither could anything
  resembling it; that target was effectively incapable of producing a finding.
- **The conventional LE floor of 0.3 rejects most of these real drugs.**
  Peptidomimetics are legitimately LE-poor, so any intuition-based LE threshold
  would have excluded the very compounds the tool is meant to rediscover.

**Change in this round:** thresholds were measured rather than assumed.
**Revised by a later round:** per-target thresholds were removed entirely, on the
grounds that a gate calibrated to a known inhibitor is anchored to the answer.
Every target now inherits one reference-free global threshold, and the accepted
cost is that it fits some targets better than others. See
[`../modules/oracle.md`](../modules/oracle.md).

## Verification

Two checks bounded the change set from both sides.

**The old output must not survive.** Re-evaluating the campaign's 10 findings
against the corrected pipeline: 9 rejected pre-dock on unstable motifs, 1
suppressed as a duplicate, **0 still hits**. The rejections happen before
docking, so that compute is not spent at all.

**Two independent mechanisms agree.** Run through the corrected *triage*, which
re-docks rather than pre-filtering, the pose-confidence tier rejects all ten on
its own without the chemistry filter's help. The ten findings scored CNN pose
0.108–0.314 against 0.80+ for the reference drugs, so a 0.4 gate separates the
populations with margin on both sides. gnina's network had been reporting that
these poses were not credible binding modes the entire time — in column 4 of
every log table the tool parsed and discarded (A1).

**Real chemistry must get through.** Rejecting the old output only proves the
gate is tighter. A single iteration against `hiv_protease` produced two hits at
-11.18 and -11.17 kcal/mol with CNN pose scores of 0.864 and 0.810, from ordinary
medicinal chemistry (add an amine, add a chlorine) on a peptidomimetic scaffold
that D2 had made unpreparable.

**The chemistry filter must not reject real drugs.** 0 of 250 approved drugs in
`seeds/approved_drugs.smi` are flagged, and artemisinin (ring endoperoxide),
paroxetine (benzodioxole), aspirin (phenol ester) and vorinostat (hydroxamic
acid) all pass — each of which a naive version of these patterns rejects.

Tests went from 92 to 149, all passing.

> **Numbers revised since.** This round reported "5/5 reference drugs pass the
> corrected oracle", measured against the per-target thresholds that existed at
> the time. After per-target thresholds were removed in favour of one
> reference-free global gate, **3 of 5 clear it**: erlotinib and nirmatrelvir do
> not. The reference-drug table was also re-measured in triplicate, which widened
> the CNN pose range to 0.598–0.981 and showed that single-run docking numbers
> overstate their own precision. Current figures are in
> [`../modules/oracle.md`](../modules/oracle.md).

## What was not changed

- **Selectivity off-target data (F2).** The metric is fixed, but no target ships
  off-target receptors. Populating them is data collection, and choosing
  off-targets is a scientific judgement worth making deliberately. This remains
  the largest gap for repurposing.
- **Docking noise (A4).** Exhaustiveness moved 4 → 8 and consensus scoring
  suppresses single-function false positives, but no replicate docking or
  variance estimate was added. A cheap re-dock of borderline candidates before
  saving a finding would be the principled fix.
- **Coverage granularity.** `interaction_types` (donor / acceptor / hydrophobic
  per residue) exists and is off by default. With union coverage now meaningful
  it would give 4× the bits and a longer-lived novelty signal, but it deserves a
  measured comparison rather than a flipped default — particularly since the
  interaction assignment is a heavy-atom approximation.
